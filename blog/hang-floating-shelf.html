Hanging a Floating Shelf on My Own, With the Level Beep On

By Raghul Babu · Published 20 October 2026

I needed to hang an 80 cm floating shelf in our living room on a weekend when nobody was home to help. The awkward part of a job like this is holding the bracket against the wall, keeping it level and marking the holes, all with two hands. So I used the feature I added to this inclinometer for exactly this situation: a beep when it's level.

What you need

  • A smartphone
  • The shelf and its hidden bracket
  • A drill, the right wall plugs and screws, and a pencil
  • A detector for pipes and cables, to check the wall before drilling
  • A spirit level for the final check

Turning on the beep

Open the inclinometer, tap the gear icon at the top right, and switch on Audio Feedback. Press Start Measuring. Each time the reading reaches level, the phone plays a short beep and the bubble turns green. "Level" here means both pitch and roll round to 0°.

How I hung it

  1. I checked the wall for pipes and cables where the bracket would go.
  2. I held the bracket against the wall at the right height and laid the phone flat on top of the bracket's bar, with its long side running along the wall. The tilt along the shelf now shows as Pitch (X).
  3. With one hand on the bracket and my eyes on the bracket rather than the screen, I moved one end up and down until I heard the beep, then marked the holes through the bracket.
  4. I drilled, plugged and screwed the bracket on, leaving the screws slightly loose, and checked again for the beep before tightening.
  5. I slid the shelf on and did a final check with a spirit level along its length.
  6. After I put books on it, I checked Roll (Y) with the phone pointing away from the wall. It showed whether the shelf was dipping forward under the weight.

Why the beep helped

The hard part wasn't knowing the angle. It was looking at a level while keeping something heavy still against a wall. With sound, my eyes could stay on the bracket and the pencil. It's the same reason the beep is useful for levelling things you can't easily see, like the top of a tall cupboard.

TipTurn on Audio Feedback before you pick up the bracket. Once your hands are full, there's no free hand to open the settings.

From RaghulGreen, or the beep, means the reading rounds to 0°, which covers anything within about half a degree. Over my 80 cm shelf, half a degree is up to 7 mm from one end to the other, and you can see that against a straight tile line. I used the beep to get the bracket close while my hands were full, then did the final check with a spirit level.
