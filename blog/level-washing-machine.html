How I Stopped My Washing Machine Walking Across the Floor

By Raghul Babu · Published 20 October 2026

After we moved flats, our washing machine started banging during the spin cycle and slowly walking out from under the worktop. After a week of pushing it back, I put my phone on top of it. It was 2° down at the front and 1° off to one side. That doesn't sound like much, but on a drum spinning at full speed it was enough. This is how I fixed it.

What you need

  • A smartphone
  • A spanner or adjustable wrench that fits the machine's feet and lock nuts
  • A second pair of hands to tilt the machine while you turn a foot (helpful, not essential)

Reading the two directions

The inclinometer shows two numbers. With the phone flat on the machine and its top pointing toward the back, Pitch (X) is the front-to-back tilt and Roll (Y) is the side-to-side tilt. A machine needs both close to zero. Mine was out in both, which is why it moved diagonally.

How I levelled it

  1. I put the phone on the floor next to the machine first. The kitchen floor itself read 1° toward the door, so I knew the feet had to make up for the floor, not just for a crooked foot.
  2. I put the phone in the middle of the machine's top, pressed Start Measuring, and noted pitch 2° and roll 1°.
  3. For each front foot, I loosened the lock nut, turned the foot to raise that corner, and checked the phone again. I adjusted a little at a time, because a quarter turn changes the reading more than you'd expect.
  4. When both numbers read 0° and the bubble turned green, I turned the phone around on the lid as a check. It still read 0° both ways.
  5. I tightened the lock nuts up against the machine body, so the feet can't creep.

If it still shakes

Level fixed most of it, but not all. A few other causes are worth ruling out:

  • Transit bolts: new machines are shipped with bolts in the back that lock the drum. If they're still in, the machine will shake violently. The manual shows where they are.
  • Small loads: a single heavy item, like a bath mat, can unbalance the drum even on a level machine.
  • Soft floors: on a springy wooden floor, the floor moves with the machine. Putting it near a wall, where the floor is stiffer, helps.

TipCheck the floor before the machine. If the floor slopes, you'll know up front how much the feet have to correct, and you won't think the phone is wrong.

From RaghulFor me, level wasn't the whole fix. The machine read 0° and 0° but still rocked, because one back foot was barely touching the floor. Press down on opposite corners: if it rocks, adjust that foot until the machine sits firm, then check the angle again. My lock nuts had worked loose, and that is probably how it got crooked in the first place.
