Building a Ramp to My Shed: Degrees, Percent and Ratio Explained

By Raghul Babu · Published 20 October 2026

Our garden shed has an 18 cm step at the door, and lifting the lawnmower and a full wheelbarrow over it every week got old fast. I decided to build a simple ramp from timber. The first thing I ran into was that every source described slope differently: degrees, percent, or a ratio like 1:12. This is how I worked it out and checked the result with my phone.

Three ways to describe the same slope

  • Ratio (1:n): for every 1 unit up, n units along. 1:12 means 1 cm up for every 12 cm along.
  • Percent: rise divided by run, times 100. 1:12 is 8.3%.
  • Degrees: the angle itself, which is what the inclinometer shows. Degrees = arctan(rise / run).

To go from the phone's reading back to the others: percent = 100 × tan(angle), and the ratio is 1 : (1 / tan(angle)).

1:205%2.9°
1:128.3%4.8°
1:812.5%7.1°
1:425%14.0°

1:12 is a common guideline for wheelchair ramps. A ramp for garden equipment can be steeper, but the steeper it gets, the harder it is to push a loaded wheelbarrow up and the more a mower wants to run away on the way down.

What you need

  • A smartphone
  • A tape measure
  • Timber for the ramp surface and cross-battens underneath
  • A calculator, or the table above

How I planned and checked it

  1. I measured the rise: 18 cm from the ground to the shed floor.
  2. A 1:12 ramp would need to be 2.16 m long, which would have blocked the path. I settled on a 1.5 m ramp: 18 / 150 = 12%, or about 6.8°.
  3. After building it, I laid the phone on the ramp pointing uphill and pressed Start Measuring. It read 7°, which matches 6.8° rounded to a whole degree.
  4. I turned the phone around for a second reading. It read 7° again (with a minus sign, because the phone now faced downhill), so my phone has no noticeable offset at this angle.

TipMeasure the rise with a tape measure first and work out the angle you expect. The phone is then a quick check that the finished ramp matches the plan, not a guess.

From RaghulMeasure the ramp in three places: bottom, middle and top. My first version sagged when I stood on it, and the reading near the top went from 7° to 9°, which is where a loaded wheelbarrow suddenly feels heavy. An extra cross-batten underneath the middle fixed it.
