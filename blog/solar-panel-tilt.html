Setting the Tilt of My Solar Panels With a Phone Inclinometer

By Raghul Babu · Published 20 October 2026

A couple of panels on adjustable tilt frames went onto our flat garage roof. The plan from PV Freund said 30°, and the frames even had 30° printed next to one of the holes. I bolted them up there, and then checked with my phone because I was curious. It said 32°. Here is why, and how I fixed it.

Why the markings were wrong

The frame markings were correct. They measure the angle between the panel and the frame's base. The problem was the roof. A "flat" roof is almost never flat: it is built with a small fall so rainwater runs off. Our garage roof falls a couple of degrees toward the gutter, and the panels faced the same way, so the roof's slope added to the frame angle.

An inclinometer measures against gravity, not against the roof, so it shows the angle the panel actually has to the sun.

What you need

  • A smartphone
  • The tilt angle you're aiming for (from your planning tool or installer)
  • A spanner or socket for the frame bolts
  • A clean, flat part of the panel frame or mounting rail to put the phone on

How I set the angle

  1. I went up in the early morning, before the panels got hot.
  2. I opened the inclinometer, pressed Start Measuring, and laid the phone on the panel's aluminium side rail with the top of the phone pointing up the slope. The panel's tilt shows as Pitch (X).
  3. First reading 33°. I turned the phone around on the same spot: 31°. Ignoring the sign and averaging gives 32°.
  4. I loosened the rear leg bolts and moved them to the next hole, which brought the panel down. I re-checked: 31° and 29°, averaging to 30°.
  5. I tightened the bolts and measured once more, because tightening can pull the frame slightly.
  6. I repeated this for the second frame. It needed a different hole, because the roof under it fell a little less.

How exact does it need to be?

Not very. Near the best angle, the yearly yield changes very little for a degree or two either way, so I didn't chase perfection on a roof. What I did want was both panels at the same angle, measured with the same phone and the same routine, so the row looks tidy and both panels behave the same.

If your panels are on a pitched roof, the tilt usually just follows the roof. In that case measuring the roof pitch is the useful step.

TipPut the phone on the aluminium frame or rail, not on the glass. The frame is a straight, rigid edge, and you won't scratch the panel.

From RaghulMeasure early or late in the day. Panel frames in full sun get hot enough that my phone dimmed its screen to protect itself, and a hot phone on hot metal is not where you want to be squinting at numbers. And don't trust the angle labels on the frame legs on a roof you haven't measured: they only know the frame, not what it is standing on.
