Levelling a Campervan on a Sloping Campsite

By Raghul Babu · Published 20 October 2026

The first time we rented a campervan, our pitch on the campsite looked flat. It wasn't. I slept with my head lower than my feet, the cupboard door kept swinging open, and in the morning the water in the kettle was visibly leaning. The next evening I used my phone to level the van properly before we got comfortable. This is how.

What you need

  • A smartphone
  • Levelling ramps or chocks (many rental vans come with a pair)
  • Someone outside to guide you onto the ramps

How I levelled the van

  1. I parked roughly where we wanted to be and put the phone flat on the floor in the middle of the van, with its top pointing toward the front. Pitch (X) is now front-to-back and Roll (Y) is side-to-side.
  2. After pressing Start Measuring, it read 3° nose-down and 2° toward the passenger side.
  3. Side to side is the harder one to fix, so I tackled it first: ramps under the two wheels on the low side. I drove up slowly while my partner watched the wheels, and I checked the phone each time we stopped. The second step on the ramps brought roll to 0°.
  4. For front to back, we turned the van around so the slope ran the other way, and that alone brought pitch down to 1°.
  5. I set the handbrake, left the van in gear, and checked the phone once more after everyone had climbed in, because people and luggage shift the van slightly.

Why level matters in a van

  • Sleep: a couple of degrees doesn't sound like much, but you notice it lying down all night.
  • Doors and cupboards: hinged doors swing open or shut on their own.
  • The fridge: some campervan fridges, especially absorption types, need to be close to level to cool properly. The van's manual or the fridge's label tells you how much tilt it tolerates.
  • Water: sinks and shower trays drain toward the outlet only if the van isn't leaning away from it.

TipMeasure on the floor, not on the table or worktop. Fold-out tables and worktops are often not parallel to the floor, and the floor is what the bed and fridge sit on.

From RaghulI put the phone in the middle of the van rather than near a wheel arch. Floors are often raised or uneven over the arches, and the middle gave the same reading as the bed area. If you can't get everything to 0°, get side to side right and leave a degree or so front to back with your head uphill. I slept far better that way than on the 'nearly level' first night.
